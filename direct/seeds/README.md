# Published seeds

`paper23.pts` transcribes the 23 integer points in Figure 1 (page 2) of
Andrew Krapivin, Benjamin Przybocki, and Marijn J. H. Heule,
*Toward Satisfiability Modulo Realizability*, arXiv:2607.02958v1 (2026).
Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02958

Its claimed property is **no empty convex hexagon and no convex heptagon**,
and its bounding box is 672 by 566. It is a published input, not a discovery
of this implementation. Experiments initialized with it must be described as
seeded refinement or extension, never independent discovery from scratch.

`es32.pts` and `es26cap5.pts` are generated by `direct/construct.cpp` from the
classical Erdős–Szekeres construction, following Sections 2.1 and 2.3 of
Frank Duque, Ruy Fabila-Monroy, and Carlos Hidalgo-Toscano,
*Point Sets with Small Integer Coordinates and no Large Convex Polygons*,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.03075 . Their exact coordinates are generated here;
the underlying existence results and method are known.

The construction joins recursively defined cup/cap blocks by translations
satisfying exact orientation inequalities, tries uniform horizontal gaps from
1 through 1000, and applies an integer vertical shear to reduce the bounding
box. At the final recursive step of each cup/cap block the horizontal separation
is reduced to one, as suggested in Section 3 of the source paper.
`--gon 7` uses all six blocks and produces 32 points without a convex
heptagon. `--gon 7 --cap 5` keeps the first four blocks, of sizes 1, 5, 10,
and 10, producing 26 points without a convex heptagon or 5-cap.

Reproduce and independently validate:

```sh
direct/construct --gon 7 --output direct/seeds/es32.pts
direct/verify --input direct/seeds/es32.pts --gon 7 --hole 0
direct/construct --gon 7 --cap 5 --output direct/seeds/es26cap5.pts
direct/verify --input direct/seeds/es26cap5.pts --gon 7 --hole 0 --cap 5
```

For the cap claim, a cap can use at most `i+1` points from its first block
`i`, and at most one point from every later block. With the first four
blocks it therefore has at most four vertices. Removing later blocks
preserves the absence of convex heptagons. The integer shear preserves
orientations and x order, so it also preserves all cap constraints.

`overmars29.pts` transcribes Figure 1 of Mark Overmars,
*Finding sets of points without empty convex 6-gons*, UU-CS-2001-07 (2001):
https://ics-archive.science.uu.nl/research/techreps/repo/CS-2001/2001-07.pdf
This is a published 29-point construction without an empty convex hexagon,
not a new discovery. Its convex hull layers have sizes 3, 4, 7, 7, 7, 1.
